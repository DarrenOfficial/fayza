i was sifting through the scattered remnants of my phone, the digital detritus of time, when i unearthed something i hadn’t touched in what felt like lifetimes. an app, forgotten yet now beckoning, as though it knew i had arrived at a place where its presence no longer felt like a wound. i opened it, and the past rushed forward like a wave—old conversations stretching back into the shadows, each word a soft echo of someone i thought i’d left behind.

and then i came to it. the one conversation that always felt like a scar beneath the skin, a line in the fabric of my life that never quite unraveled. the words that once wrapped themselves around my throat, constricting with a venomous familiarity. i could almost taste the bitterness again, the way each sentence had once bruised my being. but now, something shifted what was once a suffocating weight now felt almost weightless. who was she, the girl who had written these words? who was the one who had placed herself in the middle of the lies and deception, who had let her heart flutter like a fragile leaf in the winds of a storm she never invited? how had she trusted so fully, so blindly, when the truth had been buried deep in the earth all along, unseen and unspoken? how had she stayed, again and again, in a place where the ground always shifted beneath her, where the hands that once promised comfort only ever grasped in cold deceit?

i had spent so many nights unraveling myself, seeking the roots of my mistakes, questioning where i had faltered, where i had let the thorns of lost friendships pierce too deeply. but now, reading those words again, the puzzle pieces began to fall into place. the truth, soft and quiet as it was, whispered to me in a voice i hadn’t heard in ages “*you were never wrong to walk away*.”

***there was nothing wrong in letting go of what could not sustain you, of severing ties to the roots that poisoned the soil you had tried so desperately to nurture.***

and then, like a half-remembered dream, i recalled something that had been said to me once, long ago, *“you’ll look back at this version of yourself and laugh, you’ll laugh at how small you once seemed.”* i laughed. softly at first, and then louder, until the sound of it was almost freeing. *how strange*, i thought, how strange that i had once poured so much of myself into someone who never cared to hold the space i gave. how could i have been so tender with a heart that was not capable of tenderness, so patient with a soul that never sought to understand mine? how foolish it seemed now, how impossible that i had ever believed the darkness could somehow become light, that i could change a storm by standing in the rain.

and so i saw it. i saw the change unfold like a hidden bloom breaking through frozen earth. i had been both the caterpillar and the chrysalis, my former self writhing and shifting in the shadows, aching to break free. i was no longer the one who clung to the lies, who found comfort in the chaos, who let the world bruise her in the name of love. i had become something else, something unfamiliar, but also undeniable. the old skin had peeled away, layer by layer, leaving only the truth of me behind. the one who had once sought answers in the voices of others now found solace in the stillness, where the silence wrapped around her like a comforting shroud.

i was not broken. i was not lost. i had been becoming, always becoming. and as i stood there, i felt the soft exhale of release. the metamorphosis had not been sudden, nor had it been easy—but it had been inevitable.
