there are many ways to remember someone after they are no longer beside you. some people choose distance immediately, sanding down the details until the person becomes more idea than memory. i find i am not interested in that kind of forgetting. not because i am holding on, but because i want to remain honest about what was, at least once, deeply felt.

`so if i am to speak of you here, i want to do it through what i know i loved.`

**i loved the way you learned.** not performatively, not as something to display, but with the quiet dedication of someone who understood that understanding takes time. you allowed things to unfold at their own pace. you did not rush comprehension, nor did you seem threatened by not knowing. there was patience in you that felt increasingly rare, the kind that does not demand immediate clarity, but trusts that clarity arrives when it is ready.

**i loved your curiosity**, how it seemed less like a trait and more like an orientation toward the world. you asked questions others might have considered unnecessary. you lingered where most people skimmed. there was something almost boyish in that attentiveness, something gently nerdy in the way your interests could absorb you completely. it made the world feel textured, as if there were always another layer waiting to be discovered just beneath the surface.

to be near someone who is sincerely curious is to be reminded that life is not yet exhausted of its meanings.

**i loved your consideration**, the subtle ways it revealed itself. not grand gestures, but small calibrations and the way you paid attention, the way you adjusted, the way you sometimes anticipated what might make another person more at ease. love, when it is quiet like that, often goes unnoticed by the person offering it. but the body registers it. **mine did.**

also there were moments, simple ones, when the way you moved through the world made me feel accompanied rather than alone inside it.

**and i loved the way you tried to love**. i say tried not to diminish it, but to honor the effort that was visible even when it was imperfect. because i know that love is rarely an instinct we arrive fully fluent in; most of us are learning it in real time, translating from emotional languages we inherited long before we understood what they meant.

looking back now, i can see more clearly the depths you carried, the ones that did not always have words. the distance that sometimes appeared was not emptiness but protection, a structure built long before i arrived. avoidance is often misunderstood as indifference, when more often it is a form of self-preservation shaped in earlier rooms, during years when emotional safety was uncertain or inconsistently given.

`i recognized something of that architecture because, in different ways, i have lived inside it too.`

there is a particular loneliness in learning, as a child, that closeness can overwhelm the very systems meant to hold it. the body adapts. it becomes careful. it learns to regulate through space.

so no, **i never saw you as someone incapable of depth.** if anything, i sometimes felt the depth was precisely what made you step back, as if proximity risked touching places you had once needed to guard carefully just to survive.

understanding this does not rewrite our ending. comprehension is not the same as compatibility, and insight alone cannot sustain what two nervous systems cannot hold together.

we were, in the end, two people with histories that both connected and complicated us.

it is too late now to realize certain things out loud, too late to repair what has already completed its arc. but i find that lateness does not cancel meaning. some realizations are not given to us so that we may return, but so that we may carry them forward differently.

**and this is what loving you has given me.**

it has refined the way i listen. it has made me more attentive to the quiet languages people speak before they have words for themselves. it has asked me to look, gently but honestly, at the places within me that still needed growing. the ways i sometimes reached too quickly, the moments i mistook intensity for security, the parts of me that were still learning how to remain without disappearing.

growth rarely arrives without asking for something in return. in this case, it asked for a relationship that was both meaningful and, ultimately, unsustainable.

there is a particular humility in admitting that some of our most important teachers do not stay in our lives as partners. sometimes they first appear as lovers, only to be understood later as thresholds.

you were also, in many ways, my friend - one of the more meaningful ones to have walked beside me for a time. and while it aches slightly to know that certain forms of closeness belong now to the past, i would rather hold that ache than pretend the closeness was insignificant.

what we shared altered me. not catastrophically, not irreparably, but quietly, like water reshaping the edges of land over time.

i am no longer interested in asking whether it should have lasted longer. duration is a poor measure of significance. there are connections that span decades and never touch the deeper architecture of the self, and there are those that arrive, transform, and depart within a much shorter season.

loving you showed me that my heart is capable of patience, of perception, of staying present even when certainty was unavailable. it revealed a resilience i might not have recognized otherwise and the ability to love without guarantees and to survive the absence that follows.

for a while, i was tempted to translate the ending into personal failure, to gather my mistakes and hold them too tightly. but healing has been teaching me that relationships are rarely undone by one person alone. they are ecosystems, shaped by timing, readiness, history, and the invisible negotiations between two inner worlds.

so rather than standing here in self-blame, i choose something steadier: responsibility without cruelty.

i will take what is mine to learn. i will allow what is not mine to return to where it belongs.

and i will keep the beauty without insisting that it should have become permanence.

there is a version of maturity that knows how to release without erasing.

this is me practicing that.

months from now, perhaps even sooner than i imagine, i will read these words from a different emotional altitude. what feels tender today will likely feel spacious then. i will see more clearly that this was not simply a story about losing someone, but also about meeting a version of myself who could love with openness rather than fear.

that is not something i regret.

where you are now is no longer mine to trace, just as my path is no longer shaped by your nearness. this is not a tragedy. it is simply the natural divergence of two lives that, for a time, learned from their intersection.

still, i remain grateful.

not in the hurried way people sometimes invoke gratitude to prove they are healed, but in the quieter sense that recognizes how certain encounters expand us beyond who we were before them.

some people enter our lives not to accompany us indefinitely, but to reveal what our hearts are already capable of becoming.

***loving you did that for me*****.**

and so i leave this here. not as an attempt to hold you in place, but as a marker of a love that was real, a season that was formative, and a self who walked through it and continued.

i am still walking.

and i am carrying forward everything this love taught me about patience, curiosity, tenderness, and the kind of presence i want to keep offering the world including to myself.

that, more than the ending, is what remains.
