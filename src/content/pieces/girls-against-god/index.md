i used to believe. it felt as natural as breathing, like the air i inhaled was already filled with truth, and all i had to do was exhale in prayer. there was no doubt in it, no space for confusion. faith was like a road beneath my feet, straight and sure, leading me in one direction, towards one answer. it wasn’t something i had to question—it simply was. it was like the sun, rising every morning without fail, casting light on everything it touched. it was the rhythm of the world, the steady pulse that made everything feel right, like i was walking in harmony with something far greater than myself. belief was the foundation of everything, the map that guided my steps, the steady hand that held me up.

but slowly, over time, the road began to narrow. the more i walked, the more i noticed the stones beneath my feet, the cracks in the pavement. at first, they seemed small—just pebbles, easily stepped over. but with each step, they grew, turning into jagged rocks that caught at my heels. the certainty i once felt started to slip through my fingers like sand, and i couldn’t hold on to it anymore. the path that had once been so clear started to bend and twist in ways i couldn’t have imagined. every corner seemed to hide something new, something unfamiliar. every time i tried to take a step forward, i stumbled, unsure of the ground beneath me. the certainty i once took for granted had become something fragile, like an old book whose pages were starting to tear, leaving me with fragments instead of answers.

i began to wonder. if this path is the only one, why are there so many others that seem just as real, just as full of belief? if my faith is the truth, then what about all those who walk a different road? how can so many people be so sure, and yet, each one believes something different? how do they know they’re right? if my way leads to light, then why does it seem like so many others have their own light, just as bright, just as burning? could it be that all those lights are leading somewhere too, and maybe none of them are wrong? the more i thought about it, the more the road ahead seemed blurry, like i was walking through fog that refused to lift, and every time i reached for clarity, it slipped away, just out of reach.

i started to see the world in new ways. i began to question the things i had always accepted as facts, truths that were fed to me without a second thought. what is the meaning of suffering, if there is a God who loves us? why do good people suffer, why do children die, why is cruelty allowed to spread like fire across the world? and if there is a plan, if everything happens for a reason, then why do things happen that make no sense at all? how do you reconcile the beauty of the world with the pain it holds? how does faith survive in a world so full of contradiction? these questions echoed in my mind, each one a stone i couldn’t avoid. i wanted to keep walking, to keep believing, but with every question, the path grew steeper, and the light grew dimmer. it was like walking through a forest, unsure of which direction to turn, every tree casting a shadow that made the way forward harder to see.

the more i looked, the more i saw how fragile everything was. like a tower built on sand, belief started to feel unstable, as though it might fall if i looked too hard, too closely. i tried to ignore it, tried to push forward, but the foundation beneath me kept shifting, and i felt myself slipping. the truth that had once been my anchor seemed to float away like smoke, and no matter how tightly i clung to it, it dissolved in my hands. i wanted to believe again, to return to the comfort of what i once knew, but every time i reached for it, the pieces slipped away, as if the universe was pulling them from my hands, leaving me with nothing but the emptiness of questions.

now, i find myself lost in the quiet. it’s not the kind of silence that offers peace, but one that leaves me wondering where to go next. the silence doesn’t comfort me; it only deepens my confusion, echoing with the questions i don’t know how to answer. i used to believe with a heart full of certainty, but now that belief feels like an echo—faint, distant, fading with every passing thought. it’s as though the belief itself is drifting away, like a distant star that slowly disappears into the dark. i want to grasp onto it again, to feel the warmth of it, but i’m not sure where to look, or even if it’s still there, or if it’s just a memory of something i once held so tightly, now out of reach.

i stand at a crossroads, caught between the roads i once knew and the ones that seem to stretch into unknown distances. i used to know the way. now, i’m not sure if any of the paths lead to where i thought they did. every road i’ve considered feels like a story, one that might not end the way i imagined. and maybe that's the hardest part—to no longer know if there is a path at all, or if all the roads are just stories we tell ourselves to fill the emptiness we can’t bear to face. what if all the paths lead to different places, and none of them are right or wrong? what if the path i thought was mine was just an illusion, a reflection of what i wanted to see?

i used to believe. i still want to. but now, belief feels like a light flickering in the distance—barely holding on, disappearing every time i reach for it. it’s there, but it’s fading, and i’m not sure how to bring it back, or even if it can be brought back. i feel like i’m chasing a shadow, a feeling that isn’t real anymore, but i can’t stop looking for it. i stand here, at this crossroads, unsure of where to turn. the road ahead is shrouded in fog, and every step feels like it takes me farther from the certainty i once held so tightly. but even though the road is unclear, even though the light is fading, i still walk, still search, because somewhere inside, a small part of me wants to believe again. i want to find my way back, but i’m not sure if the path is still there, or if i’ve already walked past it.
