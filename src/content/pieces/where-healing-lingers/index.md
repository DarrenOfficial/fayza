for a long time i believed healing was something i needed to complete first, like a prerequisite to being taken seriously, to loving well, to speaking without embarrassment. as if pain disqualified me from articulation. lately, therapy has been undoing that belief slowly, almost without ceremony.

my therapist once said that healing isn’t about removing pain from the body. it’s about changing the way the body learns to live with it. pain doesn’t disappear, it gets renegotiated, recontextualized, softened at the edges. it becomes something i can sit beside instead of something that consumes the room. at first that idea frustrated me. i wanted transformation that looked dramatic. i wanted proof. but the longer i sit with it, the more honest it feels.

she told me that many people come into therapy looking for closure, for a clean ending that lets them declare themselves done. but memory doesn’t obey timelines. it revisits and loops, waiting patiently until the body feels safe enough to be felt. healing, in that sense, isn’t linear progress. it’s learning how to stay present when old sensations resurface without immediately turning against myself.

around that time, i came across a video online. it wasn’t trying to teach anything. just someone speaking plainly about how healing doesn’t move forward in a straight line and how falling apart again doesn’t negate the work you’ve already done. they said relapse isn’t a moral failure, it’s a response and i remember about it being called as… information? and then something in me loosened when i heard that. i realized how often i punish myself for reacting, for grieving again, for needing reassurance more than once.

for years i treated recurrence like regression. every familiar ache felt like evidence that i was doing something wrong, that i wasn’t trying hard enough, that i had misunderstood what healing was supposed to look like. therapy has been teaching me to see those moments differently. not as proof of failure but as invitations to respond with more care than i used to.

my therapist framed it simply. healing is a practice. not a destination you arrive at and never think about again. like taking care of a body or maintaining a relationship, it requires repetition, attention, patience, some days the work is heavy and explicit, naming things i’ve avoided, feeling emotions i’d rather intellectualize. other days the work is quieter and almost unremarkable. resting when i’m tired.,setting boundaries without justifying them, noticing when i start to disappear and gently choosing to stay.

there are days when i feel grounded, present, even surprised by my own resilience. and there are days when my body reacts as if none of that matters, when grief returns without explanation and fear settles into my chest like it never left. the difference now is that i don’t immediately panic, i don’t tell myself i’ve gone backwards. i let the feeling exist without interrogating it to death.

from what i’ve learned in therapy, healing as something lifelong doesn’t mean constant self-surveillance. it means building a relationship with myself that can hold contradiction. i can be aware and still hurt, i can be growing and still exhausted, i can love myself deeply and still need help without that love collapsing.

she told me healing doesn’t ask for calmness or emotional mastery. it asks for presence. ***it asks me not to abandon myself the moment things become inconvenient or messy.*** that staying, even imperfectly, is where most of the work actually happens.

the video echoed that idea in simpler language. healing doesn’t make you invulnerable, it makes you more honest about your limits. about what you can carry and what you need to put down. that honesty, more than positivity or closure, is what keeps things from calcifying inside the body.

so no, healing isn’t something i’ve finished or something i’m waiting to finish. it’s something that moves alongside me as my life changes. as i lose people. as i love again. as my body learns and relearns what safety feels like in different seasons.

i will keep writing and journaling, not to summarize progress or extract meaning too quickly, but to leave a record of where i am. someone learning that healing isn’t about becoming untouched. it’s about becoming more present with what remains.
