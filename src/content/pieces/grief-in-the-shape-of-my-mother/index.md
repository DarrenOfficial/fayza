i think i’m coming back to substack not because i’ve healed, but because i’ve stopped pretending i need to be healed first in order to speak. lately, i’ve been sitting with the idea of healing from the inside out where it feels slow and deeply internal. the kind of healing that doesn’t announce itself. it just quietly rearranges the way i breathe and react also the way i stay.

most of my life, my relationship with my mother was built on fear and confusion. it was abusive, mentally and physically, and for years i carried that truth like something *shameful*. i learned early on how to make myself smaller, how to stay alert and how to survive love by anticipating harm. when the person who raises me is also the person who hurts me, my nervous system grows up confused about what safety even looks like.

going to therapy with her felt impossible at first, i didn’t know how to sit across from her and talk about the past without dissolving. but we did it anyway though it was so slow and awkward. but on the other hand my therapist kept reminding me that healing doesn’t come from confrontation alone. sometimes it comes from shared presence. from building new experiences that don’t overwrite the past, but soften its grip.

after the session she suggested we spend more intentional, intimate time together. not as a test nor a repair mission. so we went out of town for two days and two nights. with no distractions and no rushing back into our usual roles.

we woke up slowly then we shared meals without tension. lingered over food, hopping from one culinary place to another like we were trying to savor life instead of escape it. we talked in fragments at first, then in paragraphs. then in confessions. we sat across from each other and named things we’d never dared to say out loud. we apologized to each other but not with excuses, not with defensiveness but with tired honesty. admitting where we failed each other, that we were both carrying pain we didn’t know how to put down.

my mother talked about her own suffering. about living under constant pressure from my late father, who was mentally abusive and extremely overprotective of me. hearing that didn’t erase what she did to me and it didn’t undo my childhood. but it complicated the story in a way that felt more human. i didn’t suddenly forgive everything, but i allowed myself to understand without self-betrayal.

at night, in the hotel room, we talked until morning. the kind of talks that blur time. we cried together, hugged without stiffness right after. sat in silence without fear, i ended up telling her about my grief, about my mental health, about how heavy it has been to carry everything alone. things she used to dismiss or misunderstand but this time, she listened, really listened. and that alone felt like something inside me unclenched.

there were small moments that meant more than the big conversations. moments where i took care of her, helping her rest, checking in on her, and moments where she took care of me in ways she never knew how to before. there was this quiet, almost sacred girlhood moment. she was showering while i sat nearby doing my makeup there was no tension. just two women existing in the same space. she then looked at me and told me i would get better, that everything would pass. and for the first time, i didn’t feel the urge to argue with hope.

when i came back, i told my therapist everything. how light and heavy it felt at the same time. how i was scared to believe it mattered. she said something i want to hold onto forever. she said, *your body is recalibrating*. that’s what i’m experiencing lately… the emotional dips, the exhaustion, the resurfacing grief all of these don’t mean i’m regressing. she said it means my system is finally feeling safe enough to release what it’s been holding.

she told me it’s okay if i don’t get better immediately. that healing allows room for setbacks. that progress doesn’t disappear just because a bad day shows up. she even said i’ve evolved a lot, which startled me. because from the inside, i often feel unsure like i’m not doing enough. like everyone else is moving forward faster than i am.

but she reminded me that grief and healing are not linear. oftentimes they spiral or even loop. they revisit old places with new eyes. sometimes growth looks like confusion, sometimes it looks like sadness returning, sometimes it looks like staying instead of running. she said what matters isn’t how quickly i feel better, but how gently i treat myself when i don’t.

maybe that’s why i’m writing again… but not to make sense of everything. not to offer wisdom. but to witness myself as i am right now. someone learning that healing doesn’t mean erasing the past. it means learning how to live with it without letting it define every breath.

this is another record of grief, of becoming. and for now, i’m letting that be enough.
