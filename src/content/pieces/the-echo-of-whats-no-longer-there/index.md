the silence that follows is indeed never an absence.

it is the weight of everything unsaid, a quiet so deep it sinks into the marrow of your bones. it stretches, an unseen hand, pressing against your ribs, drawing the breath from your chest until you wonder if you’re meant to live in it or break free from it.

the room feels smaller now, as if the walls have grown closer, squeezing the air out of the spaces between thoughts. you try to fill it with something—anything—but nothing quite fits. and still, it lingers. like smoke that doesn’t dissipate, curling itself around the edges of things, clinging to the air long after the fire has gone cold.

it isn’t the silence itself that unsettles you, but the echo of what’s no longer there.

the absence is not empty; it is full of fragments, sharp with the things you’ve tried to leave behind, yet impossible to escape. it gathers in the corners of your mind, where light no longer reaches, collecting the dust of forgotten promises, unspoken words, the sound of laughter that now feels like it belongs to someone else, someone you no longer know. you move through the world, but you are followed by this shadow of a past that never quite fades, the edges of it curling like a half-remembered dream, slipping through your fingers when you try to hold onto it.

> *the void grows heavy in the places you thought were safe, the places that once held warmth and comfort now feel as if they’ve been hollowed out.*

and you wonder if this is what loss is slow, insidious, creeping in until it changes the very shape of things. you look at the world, but it no longer feels like the same one. it’s as though someone pulled the rug out from under your feet, and now you’re standing on a different ground, one that doesn’t have the same texture, the same sense of belonging. you feel the weight of it all the way the earth under you seems to shift with every step, like the ground can’t decide if it wants you to stay or to go.

you catch yourself thinking about the moments before, when things were simpler. but even then, you can’t shake the feeling that something was always slipping through your grasp. maybe it was the way you never quite touched the same place at the same time, like trying to hold a reflection in a pool of water.

no matter how hard you tried to keep the shape intact, it always rippled, always blurred. and now, with everything stretched thin, you see it clearly: what you thought was certain, **was always just a flicker in the corner of your eye, a fleeting spark that couldn’t hold.**

the hardest part is not the absence of them, but the shape that absence takes. it hangs like a cloak over everything, its weight felt in the smallest moments, in the spaces between sentences, in the places where you expect a hand to reach for yours, but it doesn’t. it fills the spaces where they used to be, filling them with something strange, something unfamiliar, something that doesn’t belong, yet it’s hard to ignore. you find yourself reaching for things that once felt certain, but they are slippery, fading, as if time itself is erasing them from memory, leaving behind only the trace of what was, a faded outline you can no longer follow.

you learn that things don’t leave all at once. they drip away, bit by bit, like the slow leak of water from a cracked pipe. and you, unaware, move through it, thinking it’s still there, thinking you still have time. until one day, you wake up and realize it’s all gone, but the spaces are still filled with the memory of what once was. a hollow fullness that grows heavier the longer you carry it.

still there’s something almost beautiful in that weight.. it is the mark of something real, something that existed for a while, something that left its imprint on the world and on you, even if it didn’t stay. there is a strange grace in how things fall apart, in how the pieces scatter, leaving behind the shape of what was and the question of what could have been. it’s not about holding onto what’s been lost, but about finding a way to carry its echo without it breaking you.
