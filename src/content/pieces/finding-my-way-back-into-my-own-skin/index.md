i went to therapy again today, and for the first time in a while, i felt like i arrived as a whole person instead of something fragmented. i dressed very cunty (heheh) not in a costume way, but in a way that felt intentional. my hair finally did what i wanted it to do, my makeup didn’t feel like armor, and there was a quiet pleasure in recognizing myself in the mirror before i left. it wasn’t about proving i was okay or pretending i had moved on. it felt more like inhabiting my body again, as if i’d gently stepped back into it after standing slightly outside of myself for too long. somewhere between the last few sessions, the journaling, and the slow practice of self-regulation, i’ve learned how to redirect my energy though not forcefully, not with toxic positivity, but with choice. instead of collapsing into something that already happened and cannot push me forward, i’m learning how to place my attention where my life is still responding to me.

my therapist noticed before i said anything. she looked at me and smiled and said i looked alive. not “better,” not “healed,” but alive. she said the first time she saw me and said that i was present, articulate, reflective, but there was a dullness in my energy like my body had gone into conservation mode. but today she said, my affect was different. my eyes tracked the room, my posture was open, my breath wasn’t stuck in my chest. hearing that landed in a place deeper than validation. it felt like external confirmation of something i’ve been sensing quietly but didn’t trust yet. i told her back, that one of the reasons i’ve been getting better is because of our sessions. because she doesn’t rush me to conclusions or try to tidy up my grief. she lets me unfold at my own pace, and that safety has taught my nervous system that it doesn’t have to scream to be taken seriously.

today, i didn’t cry. not because there was nothing left to feel, but because my body didn’t need to overflow to communicate. instead, i regulated. i noticed my feet on the ground, my breath moving without force, the way my shoulders dropped when i wasn’t anticipating the next emotional hit. it felt unfamiliar in the best way. grief was still there, but it wasn’t flooding the room. it was sitting beside me, contained, acknowledged, not running the session. that alone felt like progress i didn’t have to intellectualize.

she said something that reframed everything for me. what i’m dealing with isn’t **abandonment issues** in the way people often **reduce it.** **it’s not rooted in a belief that i’m unlovable or disposable.** it’s an attachment injury layered with a nervous system that learned to stay alert in intimacy. my body doesn’t panic because someone leaves. **it panics because closeness, once established, was taken away abruptly, without repair, without conversation, without emotional continuity.** so instead of collapsing or shutting down, my system adapted by watching. tracking tone, timing, online presence, silence. not because i want to control outcomes, but because my body learned that disconnection happens quietly, without warning. that hypervigilance isn’t a personality flaw. it’s a survival response that once made sense.

she helped me see how this shows up in my life now. how i mistake vigilance for intuition, how i confuse monitoring with care. how my body stays one step ahead of loss so it doesn’t have to feel blindsided again. **but the cost of that is constant tension. always scanning, always bracing, rarely resting.** hearing this didn’t make me feel broken. **it made me feel understood in a way that softened something inside me. my reactions aren’t irrational. they’re patterned. and patterns can be gently unwound.**

what stayed with me after the session is how regulation changes the entire texture of my inner world. when i ground myself, when i return to sensation instead of story, i’m not becoming detached or numb. i’m becoming available to myself again. the urge to anticipate, to decode every sign, to search for certainty loosens its grip. not because the questions disappear, but because my body no longer believes it’s unsafe in the present moment. **healing, at least for me, doesn’t look like indifference.** it looks like staying present without bracing for impact. it looks like letting myself feel attractive, capable, alive, without attaching that feeling to someone else’s response.

maybe that’s what coming back to myself actually means. not erasing what happened, not minimizing the hurt, but no longer organizing my nervous system around it. **i’m still learning, still regulating, still finding my way back into my own skin.** but today felt like evidence that i’m not just surviving this. ***i’m slowly, deliberately, learning how to live inside myself again.***
